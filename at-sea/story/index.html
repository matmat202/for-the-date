Epilogue · Reading At Sea

Every dot on the map is a day’s run

Pick a date on At Sea on This Date and a year in the 1840s, then one in the 1750s. Same oceans, same winds; the later ships went half again as far between one noon and the next.

Data: the logbooks At Sea ships, from CLIWOC 2.1, the Climatological Database for the World’s Oceans (García-Herrera et al., Climatic Change 2005). A day’s run is the straight line between two noon positions on consecutive days, shorter than the zigzag a ship sailed; runs under 5 nautical miles (at anchor) and over 350 (a wrong position) are left out. Positions are as logged, by dead reckoning before chronometers were common, so any single run can be wrong. Medians, not means. This says ships got faster, not why. The program is in the repository.
